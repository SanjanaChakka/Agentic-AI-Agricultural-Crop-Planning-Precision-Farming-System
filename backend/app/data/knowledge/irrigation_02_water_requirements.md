# Crop water requirements and stage sensitivity

Seasonal water requirement ranges used by this project (mm, including
irrigation plus effective rainfall):

- rice: 600 to 1200
- wheat: 400 to 650
- maize: 500 to 800
- sorghum: 350 to 550
- cotton: 700 to 1100
- groundnut: 350 to 500
- chickpea: 300 to 450
- soybean: 450 to 700
- pigeonpea: 350 to 500
- tomato: 450 to 700
- mustard: 250 to 400
- potato: 350 to 550
- pearl millet: 250 to 400
- finger millet: 300 to 450

Stage sensitivity ranking (highest first):

- Very high: rice panicle initiation, maize tasselling and silking, wheat
  flowering and grain filling, potato tuber initiation and bulking, cotton
  squaring and boll development.
- High: chickpea flowering and pod filling, soybean pod filling, groundnut pod
  development, pigeonpea flowering, tomato fruit set.
- Moderate: vegetative growth of most cereals, mustard rosette.
- Low: establishment after emergence for crops with large seed reserves.

Rules of thumb for deficit irrigation:

- Allowing a mild deficit (about 20 to 30 percent depletion of available water)
  during a low-sensitivity stage saves water with little yield penalty and can
  deepen rooting.
- Deficit during a very-high sensitivity stage causes irreversible loss -
  reduced grain number, empty pods or small tubers.
- Crops in reproductive stages also respond strongly to excess water through
  lodging, nutrient loss and disease.
