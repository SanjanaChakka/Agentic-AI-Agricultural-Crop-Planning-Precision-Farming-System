# Wheat agronomic requirements

Soil: wheat prefers well-drained loam, silt loam, clay loam and alluvial soils.
It is sensitive to waterlogging at almost every stage; standing water for more
than 24 to 48 hours damages the crop.

pH: optimum 6.0 to 7.5; tolerable 5.5 to 8.0.

Temperature: optimum 15 to 22 degrees C during vegetative growth. Sow-soil
temperature should be at least 4 to 5 degrees C for germination. Terminal heat
above 30 degrees C during grain filling sharply reduces grain weight and is the
single most damaging abiotic stress for wheat.

Water: season rainfall of 400 to 750 mm. Total water requirement is 400 to
650 mm. In irrigated systems one irrigation at crown root initiation (about 20
to 25 days after sowing) and one at flowering normally carry the crop; under
water-scarce conditions the crown root initiation irrigation has the highest
priority.

Moisture: optimum root-zone volumetric moisture is 45 to 65 percent. Irrigation
is usually triggered below about 30 percent, with flowering and grain filling
the least forgiving periods.

Critical stages: crown root initiation, tillering, jointing, flowering and
grain filling.

Risks: yellow rust spreads faster when temperatures sit between 8 and 18 degrees
C with leaf wetness; north India leaf blight and Karnal bunt are associated
with warm, wet spells at flowering; loose smut and bunty smut are seed-borne.
Terminal heat and lodging under high nitrogen and wet soil are the main
management risks.
