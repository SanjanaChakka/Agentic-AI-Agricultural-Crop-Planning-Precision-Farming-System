# Soybean agronomic requirements

Soil: soybean prefers well-drained black soils, loam, clay loam, alluvium and
silt loam. It is intolerant of waterlogging, which causes yellowing and root
rot.

pH: optimum 6.0 to 7.5; tolerable 5.5 to 8.0.

Temperature: optimum 22 to 30 degrees C. Growth stops below 15 degrees C.

Water: season rainfall 600 to 1000 mm; water requirement 450 to 700 mm.
Soybean has a shallow, relatively dense root system and responds strongly to
conserved soil moisture.

Moisture: optimum root-zone volumetric moisture 50 to 70 percent; irrigation
trigger around 33 percent.

Critical stages: emergence, flowering and pod filling. Shortage at pod filling
reduces seed number and seed size directly.

Nutrient note: soybean fixes nitrogen through rhizobia and normally needs only
a modest starter nitrogen. It responds strongly to phosphorus, which is the
first deficiency seen on low-P soils, and to rhizobium inoculation where the
field has not grown soybean recently.

Risks: bacterial blight and brown spot spread in warm, humid weather; soybean
rust requires high humidity and warm temperatures; white mould and sudden death
syndrome follow wet periods.
