# Environmental conditions favourable for disease

Environmental monitoring can identify conditions that are favourable to a
disease, but it cannot diagnose a disease. Diagnosis requires field inspection
of symptoms plus, where appropriate, laboratory confirmation. Systems that
report "disease detected" from weather and humidity data alone are
overstating their evidence.

The correct wording is: "Environmental conditions favourable for X" or
"Elevated risk of X; field scouting recommended".

Commonly cited environmental favourability conditions:

- Warm, humid weather with leaf wetness or dew: bacterial blight and sheath
  blight in rice; early blight and Septoria leaf spot in tomato; Cercospora leaf
  spot in many legumes.
- Cool, wet weather around 8 to 18 degrees C: yellow rust in wheat.
- Cool, cloudy, humid weather with long dew periods: finger millet blast;
  white rust and downy mildew in mustard.
- Warm, wet soil and standing water: Fusarium wilt, Phytophthora blight,
  bacterial wilt, soft rot.
- High relative humidity (above roughly 85 percent) combined with leaf wetness:
  downy mildew, rusts, powdery and downy mildews broadly.
- Alternating wet and dry cycles around flowering: boll rot in cotton, fruit
  rot in tomato.
- Extended cloudiness and mild temperatures: aphid and whitefly vector
  activity, which raises virus risk.

Agronomic interpretation:

- Humidity alone is a weak signal. Combine it with rainfall, leaf-surface
  wetness proxies, temperature range and crop stage.
- A relative humidity above 85 percent at night for several consecutive days is
  a meaningful signal for mildew and rust risk.
- After heavy rainfall, conditions favourable for fungal disease usually
  persist for several days; scouting should be scheduled rather than left to
  chance.

Safety requirement: any automated finding in this category must be phrased as
environmental favourability with a recommended scouting action, and must never
be presented as a confirmed diagnosis.
