# Irrigation scheduling principles

Irrigation should be applied when the soil has given up a defined fraction of
its available water, not on a fixed calendar. Two complementary indicators are
used:

1. Soil-moisture depletion - irrigate when measured volumetric water content
   approaches the texture-specific refill trigger.
2. Crop water requirement - compare reference evapotranspiration (ET0) with
   crop coefficient (Kc) to estimate crop evapotranspiration (ETc) for the
   current growth stage.

Decision rules used by this system:

- Consider irrigation when measured root-zone moisture is at or below the
  refill trigger, the crop is at a moisture-sensitive stage, and no useful
  rainfall is expected within roughly 24 to 48 hours.
- Consider postponing irrigation when the crop is in a stage that tolerates
  deficit, soil moisture is only marginally below target, and significant
  rainfall is forecast inside the decision horizon. Irrigating immediately
  before heavy rain wastes water and can cause leaching or disease.
- Review required when inputs conflict - for example a dry sensor while a
  recent storm suggests adequate supply, or the crop stage cannot be
  determined.
- Insufficient information when the required measurements are missing.

Additional principles:

- Irrigate early morning where possible; late-day overhead irrigation extends
  leaf wetness and increases foliar disease risk.
- Application efficiency matters as much as scheduling. On clay soils, high
  application rates cause runoff; on sandy soils, high rates leach nitrogen
  below the root zone.
- Rainfall probability alone is not enough; the amount expected and the
  reliability of the forecast both matter. Light drizzle rarely replaces an
  irrigation.
- Water availability is a hard constraint. If reliable supply is limited, the
  crop and sowing date may need to change rather than the irrigation depth.

Every irrigation recommendation in this system is a decision-support output
that requires explicit human authorisation before any physical action is taken.
