# Soil pH and fertility ratings

Soil reaction (pH) governs nutrient availability more than absolute nutrient
content. Iron, manganese, aluminium and phosphorus become progressively less
available as pH rises above 7.5, while aluminium toxicity and phosphorus
fixation limit root growth below pH 5.5. Lime is generally used to raise pH and
gypsum or elemental sulphur to lower it; neither amendment should be prescribed
without a fresh soil test.

Standard agronomic pH classes used in this system:

- strongly acidic: pH below 5.5
- moderately acidic: pH 5.5 to 6.5
- neutral: pH 6.5 to 7.5
- moderately alkaline: pH 7.5 to 8.5
- strongly alkaline: pH above 8.5

Available soil-test nutrients are rated low / medium / high before a crop
recommendation is written. The bands used by this project (kg/ha of available
nutrient in the top 30 cm) are:

- available nitrogen: low below 110, medium 110-280, high above 280
- available phosphorus (P2O5): low below 10, medium 10-25, high above 25
- available potassium (K2O): low below 110, medium 110-280, high above 280

Organic carbon is a practical indicator of soil health because it improves
structure, water retention and nutrient buffering. Ratings used here are low
below 0.5 percent, medium 0.5-0.75 percent and high above 0.75 percent.

Interpretation cautions:

- Soil-test extract methods differ between laboratories, so absolute values
  are only comparable within the same method and depth.
- A single composite sample can hide within-field variability; grid sampling is
  recommended before whole-field fertiliser decisions.
- Organic matter is estimated from organic carbon; the two are not
  interchangeable numbers and should not be reported as the same quantity.
- pH measured in water and pH measured in calcium chloride differ by roughly
  0.5-0.6 units, so the reporting method matters when comparing records.
