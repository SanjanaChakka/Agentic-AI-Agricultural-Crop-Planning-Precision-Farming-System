# Heavy rainfall, waterlogging and dry spells

Heavy rainfall is commonly defined in short-duration terms. Thresholds used by
this system:

- 50 mm in 3 days: heavy rainfall - agronomic impact likely.
- 80 mm in 3 days on fine-textured soils: waterlogging risk.
- 100 mm in 3 days: severe rainfall event - expect runoff, erosion and lodging.

Consequences of excess water:

- Waterlogging reduces soil aeration; root respiration falls within hours and
  visible wilting can occur in a wet field, which is a classic diagnostic trap.
- Nitrogen denitrification and leaching occur rapidly on sandy soils; apply
  nitrogen only after the excess water has drained.
- On heavy clay and black soils, a wet spell after a dry period destroys soil
  structure and reduces infiltration for subsequent irrigations.
- Fungal and bacterial diseases that need free water or high humidity increase
  sharply after a wet spell.
- Lodging risk rises in cereals with high nitrogen status.

Extended dry period: 14 or more consecutive days with forecast rainfall below
2 mm and reference evapotranspiration above 3 mm per day is treated as a dry
period. Consequences are exhaustion of soil water reserves, loss of
germination and crop establishment, greater irrigation demand and higher
wind-related evapotranspiration.

Cloudy, calm weather with high humidity suppresses transpiration and can
deplete soil moisture more slowly than expected; conversely, hot windy
weather can exhaust reserves in a matter of days.

Forecasts carry genuine uncertainty. A decision-support system should state the
expected rainfall amount and probability, note when the forecast is short or
low-confidence, and avoid presenting a single deterministic rainfall number as
a guarantee.
