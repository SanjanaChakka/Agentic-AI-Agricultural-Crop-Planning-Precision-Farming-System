# Potato agronomic requirements

Soil: potato prefers friable, well-aerated sandy loam, loam, silt loam and red
soils. Clods and compaction restrict tuber expansion and increase deformities.

pH: optimum 5.0 to 6.5; tolerable 4.5 to 7.0. Soil pH above about 7.0 raises
the risk of tuber blotch (common scab relative), and above 7.5 increases zinc
deficiency.

Temperature: optimum 15 to 22 degrees C. Tuber bulking stops above 28 degrees C.

Water: season rainfall 500 to 750 mm; water requirement 350 to 550 mm. Soil
moisture must stay fairly high because tubers are shallow rooted, but waterlogging
causes soft rot.

Moisture: optimum root-zone volumetric moisture 55 to 75 percent; irrigation
trigger around 40 percent.

Critical stages: sprout development, stolon growth, tuber initiation and bulking.
Stress at tuber initiation cannot be compensated later.

Risks: late blight is favoured by cool, wet, humid weather and is the dominant
risk in humid potato regions; early blight follows warm, humid conditions;
black scurf and common scab are soil-borne and are aggravated by liming to
neutral pH.
