# Crop growth stages and tied agronomic operations

Field preparation:

- Tilth to 15 to 20 cm, a firm, level seedbed and free of large clods.
- On black soils, deep ploughing once every 2 to 3 years improves infiltration
  and root penetration. On sandy soils, minimal tillage conserves moisture.

Sowing and seed treatment:

- Sow on time. Every week of delay usually costs more than any input that can
  be added later.
- Treat seed for 15 to 30 minutes before sowing for crops with seed-borne
  disease risk.
- Maintain the recommended plant population; thinning after establishment
  wastes water and nutrients.

Nutrient application:

- Full phosphorus and potassium basal, nitrogen split across the season.
- The largest share of nitrogen should be applied at the crop-specific
  high-sensitivity stage, not entirely at sowing.
- Do not apply extra nitrogen to a waterlogged field; it is largely lost.

Irrigation operations:

- Critical-stage irrigations carry the yield; irrigate on soil-moisture
  evidence, not habit.
- Prefer drip or furrow over flood where water is scarce, and never irrigate
  overhead in the evening.

Crop observation and scouting:

- Walk a W-pattern across the field at least weekly, inspecting at least five
  points and recording both pests and disease symptoms.
- Scout after rain events and after irrigation; symptoms are most visible
  within 24 to 72 hours of a favourable weather window.
- Record stage transitions so that stage-sensitive advice stays correct.

Harvest planning:

- Plan harvest about two weeks before expected maturity.
- Harvest immediately after a dry spell; rain at harvest causes sprouting,
  grain mould and quality loss.
- Record actual yield by plot so that the next season's plan is evidence-based.
