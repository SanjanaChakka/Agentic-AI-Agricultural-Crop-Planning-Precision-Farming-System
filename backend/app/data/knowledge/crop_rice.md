# Rice agronomic requirements

Soil: rice tolerates a wide range of textures but performs best on heavy clay
and clay loam soils with good water-holding capacity. Sandy soils need heavier
irrigation frequencies.

pH: optimum 5.5 to 7.0; tolerable 5.0 to 7.5. Strongly alkaline soils above 8.0
are unsuitable because iron and manganese become unavailable.

Temperature: optimum 24 to 32 degrees C for vegetative growth. Grain filling is
damaged by heat above 35 degrees C and by cold spells below 15 degrees C.
Germination is poor below 20 degrees C.

Water: season rainfall of 1000 to 3000 mm is typical. Total water requirement is
600 to 1200 mm depending on season and method; the crop has very low water-use
efficiency compared with other cereals and is frequently grown under puddled
irrigation.

Moisture: rice tolerates saturation. Volumetric soil moisture between 45 and 90
percent is acceptable; below roughly 25 percent the crop enters severe water
stress, which is unusual outside a managed drought.

Critical stages: panicle initiation, booting, flowering and grain filling are
the most sensitive. Moisture stress at panicle initiation lowers tiller and
panicle number and cannot be recovered later.

Risks: bacterial blight and sheath blight spread rapidly under warm, humid,
standing-water conditions. Tungro is favoured by high humidity and cloudy
weather. These are environmental favourability statements only - field
inspection and laboratory diagnosis are required before any disease is
named.

Management notes: alternate wetting and drying saves 15 to 25 percent of water
and cuts methane emissions. Direct seeded rice is more exposed to weed pressure
and early moisture stress than transplanted rice.
